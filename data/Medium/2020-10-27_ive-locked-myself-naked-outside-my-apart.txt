I’ve Locked Myself (Naked!) Outside My Apartment

Daniele Ihns

I’m at that age where I’m old enough to know better. Yet, just last week, I locked myself (naked!) outside my apartment.

It was 6 o’clock in the morning and my REM sleep was interrupted by an urgent need to pee. I got up from my bed, 90% asleep, 100% au natural, and rushed to the bathroom (yes, I live alone and during the night it can get really hot when the radiators are on so I sleep without clothes).

Consumed in a new-born style of anger, I smashed closed the door behind me and proceeded to sit on the loo.

Except the loo wasn’t there. I went through the wrong door and was now standing in nature’s garb outside my front door.

As the adrenaline started to kick in, I became 100% awake to the fact that my elderly neighbors would soon be making their way down those stairs, fully dressed, and that would be it. They would take one look at me and my life would be over.

“Oh! The horror!” I could hear them saying, inside my head, in their posh elderly accents.

PANIC. I was desperately trying to re-enact the mindfulness exercises from leadership training and calm my mind just enough so I could think my way out of this situation. But, trust me, there’s no such thing as a naked leader in the corridor at 6 am.

The need to pee was too intense for me to sit down and cry so I ran down to the garbage room in the cellar, trying to dodge as many security cameras as I could on the way.

I opened each of the trash containers looking for anything I could use to hide my shame. A bin bag, an old rag, a cardboard box. Anything would have worked at this point. But nothing prepared me for what I was about to see.

An overcoat! An overcoat?!! An overcoat!!!!

Someone had thrown away a perfectly good black woolen overcoat in the bin exactly when I needed it the most.

It couldn’t be. Something was wrong. Was I still dreaming?

No, I wasn't dreaming. This was actually happening.

I took the coat and ran across town, barefoot, to my ex-boyfriend’s house to pick up the spare key that was still sitting there since we broke up months ago. It’s Autumn. I live in the mountains. It snowed last week.

My ex lives just 10 minutes away but I had to go past the school and across the central park to get there. By now it was already that time when kids are being dropped off and joggers are out and about. They looked at me in a strange way. I couldn’t feel my feet. But at least I had my overcoat.

It’s hard to describe that feeling, a mixture of gratitude with “why me??”

Please pause and try to picture yourself in this situation for a moment.

No, you can’t, because you’re a fully dressed privileged kid who doesn’t know what it’s like to have to explain to your ex why you ended up naked in your corridor at the break of dawn!

The horror! The horror!

We don’t talk enough about plain stupidity…
