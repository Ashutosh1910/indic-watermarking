Darkness and Life

Esther Spurrill-Jones

Shri walks carefully, resisting the urge to hurry. The glowing grasses alongside the path beckon her, their beauty enticing. She tries not to look directly at the light, keeping her eyes on her feet and the safe grey gravel. Her hands tremble and her knees are weak.

Beyond the light, ancient gnarled trees in staggered rows stand tall among the shadows. Soft darkness coils around trunks and branches. Winds whisper and sigh through the leaves.

A moth flutters past and, for a moment, Shri is distracted by the play of golden light across creamy wings. Then it lands on a blade of luminescent grass. A flash, a pop, and it is gone, leaving only a lingering scent like that of a snuffed candle slowly fading into the cool night air.

Shri flinches, her body tensing in anticipation and fear, even though she is in the centre of the path, nowhere near the deadly light. She stops and takes a few calming breaths before she continues. The beauty of the grasses has intensified, strengthened by the moth’s tiny life, and the call rings silently in her ears, whispering to her to step into the light. The moth only whetted its appetite. It wants her.

Shri curls her shaking fingers into her palms and keeps her head down. She could have worn heavy boots and gloves and protective clothing, but none of that could protect her. Nothing can protect against it. The only defense is to resist its call. At the edge of her vision, the radiance waxes and wanes like a heart beating. A tickle in her mind calls to her soul. A drop of cold sweat trickles down her spine.

Directly ahead of her, a figure slowly materializes in the centre of the path. Gadise waits just ahead, cloaked in shadows. The light shrinks away from her, away from the tendrils of darkness that twine around her. Shri’s feet pause of their own accord at the sight. She did not expect help on this journey, but Gadise’s assistance is always welcome. Shri may be weak to the call of the light, but Gadise stands untouched.

Shri stands before Gadise, and finds her shaking has stilled. Her fear is calmed in the depths of Gadise’s dark eyes. Gadise lifts her hands and shadows stream between her fingers, warding off the light. The siren calls fade as Shri keeps her eyes on Gadise. “You’re here,” Shri whispers in disbelief.

“Hello, Shri.” Her voice is rich and dusky, her lips merlot. She is power and joy. She is darkness and life. Gadise takes Shri’s hand, her fingers dark against Shri’s pale gold skin. She embraces Shri, enfolding her in shadow.

They come together in a flare of shadow and bright. All around them, the malevolent light cries out to her, but Shri no longer hears it. She is safe.
