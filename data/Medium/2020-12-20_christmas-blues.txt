Christmas Blues

unknown

For me, Christmas is just another day.

I swear, I’m no Grinch. I actually like Christmas season. I think the decorations are beautiful — I thoroughly love walking through city streets bathed in golden light, with LED candy canes floating on the buildings around me. I love buying gifts for other people, and giftwrapping serves as one of my therapies for perfectionism.

But like, Christmas Day is what really gets me down.

I’m not an anomaly, in that sense. Tons of people don’t look forward to annual forced, awkward family reunions. It’s not uncommon to feel dread in seeing people that you are obligated to love and be kind to, just because you share some DNA. I’m fortunate enough that that’s not the case for me. It’s not the family tension that really bother me, more so the social aspect. Again, I’m not special, but that’s how I feel.

In my family, I’m the forgotten middle child. Crushed between the responsible, beautiful older sister and the adorable, spoiled younger cousin, I was forgotten.

One of the most distinct family memories I have is from a Christmas celebration when I was in sixth grade. My Aunt Helen, whose family resided in South Korea, had flown over to the United States for that year, so it was a big deal. She entered the house with suitcases stuffed with gifts and snacks to hand out, and everyone was excited to receive their own. When it was my sister’s turn, she was handed a beautiful backpack — not one of those chunky, heavy school backpacks that get thrown around lockers and classrooms, but a delicate, floral patterned backpack that suited an outing or picnic date in a garden. My younger cousin, who was only about ten years old at the time, was gifted a sweet, pastel pink messenger bag, with anime and cartoon characters printed all around it. I waited in the background somewhat impatiently, my chest bursting and my fingers tingling, the anticipation of what my gift might be literally eating me alive.

My aunt didn’t even acknowledge me.

Sometimes I still wonder why she didn’t. Don’t get me wrong, my aunt isn’t a bad person. In fact, I now go visit her home over summer for a month, and she’s genuinely one of the most giving people I know. I owe her so much now, but this moment still stands.

Maybe she had forgotten about me that Christmas? I mean, she had 3 other siblings, her parents, and all her in-laws to tend to as well. I have other cousins that came to that family gathering. I wouldn’t put it past her to have accidentally glossed over my name in the list, only to realize on Christmas Day that she had fallen one gift short. But she didn’t even mention anything. Was it embarrassment? Apathy? I really don’t know.

All I know is that that Christmas, I was insignificant.

I know it’s immature and stupid of me to base all my Christmases based on this one, meaningless event, but I really can’t help it. Some things stick around you like the Ghost of Christmas Past, and mine happens to be the fact that I never got my own purse from my Aunt Helen.

Cheer up though, Christmas is here.
