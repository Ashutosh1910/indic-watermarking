“I was all like…”

Eli Magers

Dennis: Wait… you bought a baby from Trader Joe’s?

Alex: What? No. I said I bought barbecue from Trader Joe’s for the cookout.

Dennis: Oh! I was all like… whaaat, you know?

Alex: Ha, yeah. Anyway, could you bring―

Dennis: No. Like… I was all like… whaaat, you bought a baby!? That’s so crazy! How could you buy a baby!? I cannot believe a word of what this guy just told me!

Alex: Yeah, good thing you just misheard me. Anyway―

Dennis: Like, I was all like, I don’t know what I’m supposed to do right now. Should I report my friend to childcare services? Does this mean Trader Joe’s is cancelled again? like…

Alex: I know wild. But, could you please pick up―

Dennis: I was all like, you know what? I don’t even care, I love Alex and I’m ready to commit to being a dad with him. I don’t care that the baby isn’t mine, we’re going to be the best dang dads this big baby ever had, unlike my dad!

Alex: What?

Dennis: Like, I was all like, maybe this baby will be so inspired by our teamwork that they will grow up to be a renowned author and write a memoir about our lives. Which also means we should probably enroll them at Reigmont Schools, my cousin went there and he’s like a super big brain poet or something so they have to have a good creativity program.

Alex: Will you please stop talking for one moment―

Dennis: Then, like, I was like all like, but my cousin’s family are part of the super rich elite and Reigmont Schools is super expensive which means I need to pick more shifts up at the post office and I don’t know if I want to do that because I’m trying to get my Movie Reviews podcast off the ground. Is this perils of responsibility culminated into one decision based on sacrifice? I can’t do this because I have so much I’m working towards in my life but I already promised Alex that I’ll be a dad with him. What is a morally justifiable reason for backing out of this that will save me some face? Should I tell him I’m not feeling well and can’t ever show up to help with the baby? No, that’s too long term. I know, I’ll fake my death!

Alex: Wow.

Dennis: I was all like, I’ll borrow his car one day and drive it into the reservoir and I’ll replace my body with a bunch of straw wearing my favorite collard shirt. But, then I’ll have to get rid of my favorite collard shirt! There has to be sacrifices made somewhere, I guess. If everyone thinks I’m dead that means I can live out my life in a cabin in the Adirondack mountains like I’ve always wanted! Wait ― uh-oh! That means I’ll have to carry plenty of buckets of water in case I run into a mountain warlock, they hate water!

Alex: Hey, man. I’m actually gonna get out of here right now.

Dennis: Wait, don’t go! I actually have a crippling loneliness because nobody talks to me for some reason. You’re my only friend!

Alex: Wow. I’m so sorry. I had no idea. When you put it like that, I guess I can stick around.

Dennis: Wait… you want to stick a clown?

Alex: That’s not at all what I said at all.

Dennis: Oh! Because, I was all like… whaaat, you know?
