The question Shakespeare enthusiasts are sick of answering

Better Morning Reading

Spoiler: It’s authorship

Recently moderators on the subreddit dedicated to Shakespeare discussion introduced an interesting new rule: no more asking whether William Shakespeare really wrote all the plays historically attributed to him.

There Is No Authorship Question There are a number of other subs that discuss the various theories that someone other than the man from Stratford wrote the works attributed to William Shakespeare. This is not that place.

Apparently this is a very popular question stemming from the fact that many details of Shakespeare’s era have simply been lost to the sands of time. But it seems that there likely isn’t much left to discover, and the folks who hang out on that subreddit either feel sick of having the same conversations, or just think that the unanswerable question is merely a distraction from exploring the works themselves.

It’s also not a particularly new question nor one that lends itself well to debate on an internet forum. Going back to the 1700s, scores of books have been written on the topic (though, nobody seemed to seriously doubt Shakespeare as the author for about two centuries). In fact, the debate is old enough that followers of competing theories of who the real author was have waged campaigns against others. Societies have been formed to find the answer and feed the debate.

The real answer is the same as any, isn’t it? We’re pretty sure things are as they seem (i.e. William Shakespeare is really the author) but we just don’t know.

Anyway, here are some reasons to doubt why Shakespeare actually wrote Shakespeare:

There no paychecks or contracts for the any of the plays with William Shakespeare’s signature

No documentation of Shakespeare having been an author: written records detail more mundane transactions, like his pursuits as an investor and real estate collector

Many people in the Elizabethan England used others to promote their work to avoid consequences from restrictions to free speech

Shakespeare wasn’t educated enough to have insight into law, royal affairs, the inner relationships of high class society

Both of Shakespeare’s parents were probably illiterate

The female voices in the plays are too convincing to have been written by one man

There is no evidence Shakespeare ever left England, and his plays contain many details of foreign lands

There was no public mourning for him when he died

His will, which included inheritances given to many people who were close to him doesn’t include a single book

His surname was spelled inconsistently (sometimes as “Shake-speare” or “Shak-spear”) in both literary and non-literary documents, with the most variation observed in those that were written by hand.

Other smart people including Mark Twain, Hellen Keller, Sigmund Freud, Walt Whitman, Charlie Chaplin, Henry James, Malcolm X, and Orson Welles have publicly doubted Shakespeare as the author
