White

Tai Brite

A blank canvas stood in front of me, mocking my skills as I stared into it. My mind had somehow copied the white color, every idea having run away. Pushing a bit of color through my brain, my hands poured a glob of paint onto the palette. I thought the sudden burst of color against a white background would somehow stimulate my senses and creativity would wash over me, my excited hands rushing to keep up. But it didn’t and everything stayed stale shades of white. Uninspired with an oncoming headache, I raised my stiff hand, the tip of the brush colored orange. Carried by an empty mind, a line was drawn across the canvas, others following suit. As bright orange covered white, an idea graciously offered itself up. Maybe if I thought about the meaning of orange, I could pour the same meaning onto the canvas. Maybe I could contrast colors with blue, pulling together the endless evening sky and the surface of a deep ocean. Even with these few ideas my hand wouldn’t move. The extent of my imagination reached far beyond my arms length, always running ahead in a sprint, never close enough to grasp. My insecurities stopped my motions as well and brought upon a mountain of questions I wouldn’t and couldn’t answer. I never gave it much thought- my innate talent and rate of improving- instead opting to lay in bed and sleep. I wanted to paint. I wanted to be talented. But the pressure of my white mind never subsided. It was odd to me, how I wanted nothing more than to create something amazing but I stayed uninspired and talentless, sitting in front of my orange canvas.

. . . . .

Inside a gallery, a disheveled figure stood motionless, staring into the painting hung on the wall. Dim lights hung around her, accentuating the longing in her eyes. Her hand reached out, as if to touch the great work of art and stopped short, a sign beneath it mocking her hanging hand. It fell to her side with a thump. A few moments passed, quiet and heavy, the weight of her apparent sadness pulling on her shoulders. A closer look revealed everything drooped in a depressing way- her eyes downturned, mouth falling at the corners, and hair thin and pin straight. A strange tear rolled from her eye, straggling alone and running to her chin. A thin finger quickly wiped it away, her eyes closing for a moment before she shifted. One foot fell behind the other, dragging her away from the painting. A slow turn pulled her away, her eyes finally leaving the painting to face forward. The dim lights left her face, along with the light in her eyes. She was a blur of black and white, color having drained from her being completely. There was nothing inspiring about her, nothing she seemed to be inspired by herself. Lifeless and dim, she left the gallery, home to stare at another white canvas.
