Job Enjoyment vs. Pay: What Can You Sacrifice?

Alexandria Hoover

An open letter

Have you ever had to weigh the choices of good pay and an enjoyable job? Have you had to determine if the commute, long hours, burnout, or work was worth it? Have you ever decided “no?”

Then this letter is for you.

I know what it’s like to dislike your job but recognize the pay gets you by. I know what it’s like to think you were going to like something you ended up not liking at all. I know what it’s like to want, no need out, but you’re unsure what will happen next.

I used to think I could handle any job as long as the pay was good and I didn’t have any qualms about the business’ ethics. Until I encountered the actual dilemma. I had a much longer commute than I was used to, which piled on the mileage to my already used car. I had a job that, by its description, was exactly what I had gone to school for. I had an income I wasn’t accustomed to, allowing me to quickly pay off some debts, pile some money into savings, and even treat myself occasionally. At first, that longer commute was fun. It was my first “big girl” job, and I felt like an adult. At first, I was excited by this idea that, after years of school, I’d get to work in a real office of my own. But that excitement quickly dwindled.

Two months in, my personal life took a nose dive. Three months in, I hated how exhausted I was because of the commute. Four months in, I didn’t feel I was using my education or knowledge at all. I wasn’t permitted creativity or autonomy in things I was told I would be. By six months in, I’d had enough. But now I had to play that dangerous game: was the pay worth the depletion of my mental health?

Eight months in, I decided “no.”

I didn’t have any full-time job prospects, but I knew I couldn’t do it anymore. It wasn’t worth the lack of sleep, the anxiety, or the tears. It simply wasn’t worth sacrificing my mental health (or my happiness) for pay.

I’m telling you this because I want you to know it’s okay to make that decision. In fact, if you’re anything like me, and those around you feel the full force when you’re angry or frustrated, they will probably thank you. Make choices that are good for you and even good for those around you (you know, like your family).

If you’re having to weigh pay and job enjoyment, think about what you can sacrifice. If you can sacrifice some enjoyment, that’s totally fine. But if you absolutely can’t, and the pay is the only good thing you can see, then it’s okay to move on.

You’re the only one who can make that decision though. You’re the only one who knows you.

I can’t promise that everything is going to turn out perfectly. I still only have part-time jobs. But they are jobs I thoroughly enjoy, and I wouldn’t go back at all. I can’t promise you’ll have a new job lined up in a week. And I can’t promise the universe will suddenly make sense. But I can promise you will feel much better about yourself.
