In A World Of Our Own Making

Nitya Muralidharan

Our imagination is a strong force, it is unique to us and we can never expect someone else to understand it completely. As a child, I had a recurring dream. I had just learned to float on water with my hands and legs absolutely still, in my dreams I could do the same thing in the air, for a very long time this was a very convincing dream, I was always floating on my ceiling and it felt like the most natural thing to do. But I did not share it with anyone else. In my world, it was absolutely possible, but I knew the moment I voiced it out I might never float in my dreams.

We guard our imagination, the stuff of our dreams, and those bits about us that make us unique. And slowly we guard it so much that one day it might cease to exist unless we have written them down as a child or codified it in some way that has a presence even when we become adults.

Not all of us have to have to do something with our imagination, but we need to be able to get in touch with our imagination from time to time. The world is not patient enough for our imagination unless we learn to tap into it for a living, it's a rare a complex friend, appearing occasionally, and at times in the most bizarre ways.

In India we are rarely encouraged to run wild with our imagination, it does not fit into the plan of making a decent living when millions of others are trying to do the same thing. Having an imagination is hazardous if you are trying to crack an entrance exam where millions are focused.

So we toss it out, convince ourselves we did not have any creative gifts, to begin with, and are lucky to be a part of an analytical and rational education that might yield a decent job in the future. And we land that decent job and have that decent life, but then something seems missing.

But given how rusty our imagination has become it is not easy to coerce it out under all those rational and logical thoughts.

It's a firefly trapped in a world of worker bees.

And then we coax and plead in our own way, listen to songs which moved us, go to those places that transformed us and meet those people who did not ridicule our imagination.

And one day it slowly emerges from the debris of dead thought, but careful because it has been neglected for a long time, this time it needs care, protection, and nurturance.

Rational thought is high and mighty and tries to push back our imagination, but now this is no longer a child’s imagination, but an adult’s, it has grown a pair of wings.

It is strong enough to stand up to rational thought and see eye to eye if you let it. And so when that imagination firefly comes out nurture and protects it fiercely, for it can lead us to a world of our own making.
