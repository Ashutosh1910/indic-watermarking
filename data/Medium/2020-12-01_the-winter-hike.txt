The Winter Hike

Matthew Donnellon

He got up early that morning.

The air had a chill and the ground was covered in frost. The sun was just starting to rise

There were small clouds trailing from his mouth and nose with each step he took. The cold was more bone chilling that he thought it would be. He had a worn brown leather coat to cut the wind, but only a light sweater underneath and winter’s icy fingers were clawing down to his bones. He wore jeans too which did not work well in the cold, and his leather boots leaned to the fashionable side rather than functional.

He hope it would get warmer as the sun came up. If not it would make for a long day.

He walked along the old country road, now mostly frozen, but there were still loose stones that scraped and tumbled as he walked. Before too long he ducked off the road onto a connecting trail. There were cracking sounds as he made his way over two old frozen puddles, the thin veneers giving way to the slightest touch.

He heard a rustle in the trees and immediately regretted leaving his bear spray at home.

His fears were assuaged when a big bodied buck lumbered out of the woods, his huge rack of antlers above his head like wilderness royalty. He saw the beast a thought how clever it must be to have survived so many hunting seasons.

The deer looked at him, as if waiting to see if he posed any threat, and then quietly wandered off as casually as she strode in.

The man kept going, delighted that the rising sun had indeed removed some of the chill.

Finally he saw the break in the trees that signaled his destination.

The old cemetery in the woods originally gave him an eerie feeling the first time he came across it while hiking, but his curiosity got the better of him and he checked it out, and he was rewarded for his interest.

A young woman was waiting for him when he arrived.

“Hey there,” he said.

“I was beginning to think you weren’t going to come,” she said playfully.

“Oh there was no way I’d miss out. Though dates are usually at night.”

“I’m not your usual girl.”

“I’ll say.”

“Now what’s that supposed to mean?” she said doing her best to fake anger.

“Just this is a rather grim place to meet up.”

“Well I sort of like the seclusion. Besides you can’t beat the company,” she said gesturing to the grave markers that filled the little cemetery.

“Are you sure they won’t mind?”

“I don’t think they’re listening.”

“Well that’s good. I’m nervous enough around you I don’t need an audience.”

She laughed, “Why would you be nervous?”

“For obvious reasons.”

“Such as?”

“I think you know.”

“I know. I’m so adorable it’s disarming.”

“I’ll say.”

“Did you bring the stuff for the picnic?”

“Of course.”

“No are you sure you’re okay having it here?”

“Do I really have a choice?”

“Well seeing how ghosts can’t leave no you really don’t.”
