Gorgeous Women, Stunning Photos

Nikki Tate

Canmore is chock full of climbers (and skiers, mountain bikers, hikers, kayakers, and, yes, a few tourists). I’m here because of the climbing: a few years ago I fell in love with a climber and we moved here together so it would be easier to climb even if all the time we had was a few hours in an afternoon.

How cool to see one of my favorite local climbers, Rebecca Ferri (I know her as Bexx) exploring her artistic side in this fabulous exhibition.

These intimate portraits capture the essence of what it means to be a strong woman. [Nikki Tate]

Taking her inspiration from Brené Brown’s book, Braving the Wilderness, Ferri explores strength and vulnerability, determination and community, and beauty and confidence through her striking black and white photographs.

These are terrific portraits — each bringing out the unique qualities of the women featured.

Each image is presented alongside the woman’s answers to a series of questions. [Nikki Tate]

Interviews Add Another Dimension

While an image conveys a great deal, the accompanying commentaries provided by the women add another layer of complexity as they share in their own words what climbing means to them, the challenges they face being women climbers in what was traditionally a male domain, and a little about themselves outside the climbing world.

While some women have been climbing for decades, others are new to the sport having taken it up relatively late in life. Yet others are youngsters, just starting out (the thirty-one women featured range in age from nine to seventy).

The show runs until August 28. It’s upstairs at Elevation Place and is free and open to the public during EP’s regular hours. [Nikki Tate]

There were lots of familiar faces in the mix (Canmore is a small town) but there were also plenty of women I have yet to meet.

Their stories are by turn touching (climbing, for some, was a critical element in the path to healing), inspiring, and motivating.

Powerful Design

Ferri has done an excellent job of teasing out common threads through the series of questions she asked and recurring themes in the text were beautifully reflected in the simple design of the exhibition as a whole.

The choice of a simple black background and consistent lighting brings the images of these different women together and you know immediately these women are part of a sisterhood.

The final question asked of each woman was open-ended and it is in these final paragraphs that some of the most intimate and touching stories were shared.

I found myself thinking I could be friends with all of these women! [Nikki Tate]

As an author, I found myself thinking — Book! Book! I hope this is just the beginning of a new chapter in Ferri’s world. I would love to see her complete another series of photos and continue to collect stories of women climbers.

These images would make a fabulous coffee table book… just saying!
