A Chilling Reunion

J.A. Taylor

She came to fix what was broken

Image public domain from DigitalComicMuseum

“Right out of a cryo-capsule,” the Overseer said, gnawing his tofu burger. “A lovely damsel in distress.”

Adam’s ears perked up. “What’s that?” he asked, taking a seat with the crew and popping a tot-crisp in his mouth.

“You haven’t heard?” a crewmate asked.

“Don’t tell me we picked up someone from that god-forsaken rock?”

The crewmen all snickered. “You gotta get out of engineering more,” one of them said.

Adam raised an eyebrow. “Spit it out, Wexman.”

“Looked like she was dolled up for a date. Red dress. Perfect hair. Lipstick.”

“There’s humanoids down there?” Adam asked.

Wexman nearly choked on his laugh. “Not ‘down there…’” He wiped his mouth on his sleeve. “And not out there,” he waved toward the window. “In cold storage.” Wexman pointed toward the lift. “Hidden in plain sight this whole time.”

“She dressed up for cryo?” Adam whistled. “Must be desperate for some fellas to lift her skirt.” He chewed his food and waited for a laugh, but it didn’t come. He pushed his thin wit one step further. “How long you think it’ll take her to go through the crew?” An eery silence crept throughout the mess hall. As Adam looked up, all eyes were glued on him. “What?”

The crewmen’s heads stiffened, but their eyes flickered toward the Overseer who drew a paper from his pocket. “Found a note on her pod,” the large man said. “Care to see?” He slid the piece of paper across the table. Adam opened it.

AF, if you are reading this, you’ve found me. AF, your life will be in danger. That’s why I hid myself. We both know it didn’t end well. Wake me up. Let’s restore what was lost, fix what is broken. — Eva

Adam froze.

Eva? Only she calls me ‘AF’. Here? Impossible. She refused to come. He bit his lip. Maybe she forgave me? Said she loved me…

“Wake up, man!” Wexman shouted.

Adam regathered his thoughts. “Who is AF?” he asked, pretending to chew. He tucked his chin to his chest.

A contemptuous smile widened the Overseer’s face. “That answer’s right under your nose.”

Adam lifted his head. All eyes fixed on his nametag, which read: A. Flannagan.

Adam bolted from his seat, but the crewmen rose and secured his arms.

“Wanna see her?” the Overseer asked. At his whistle, Eva strolled in. Adam’s jaw dropped. “And Flannagan — no worries about getting your story straight. She offered to marry whoever brought you to justice.”

The Overseer bit a hole out of a small round pickle, then slid it over his finger. “Think this will work for my wedding ring?”

“Yes, darling,” Eva cut in. “And least I know you like the dress.”

Hands on her hips, she twisted her svelte figure toward Adam and rested her fingers on her cheeks in mock worry. “AF, your life is in danger! But don’t worry — the Overseer will fix what is broken.”
