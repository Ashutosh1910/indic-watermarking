What is Beauty….?

Richard Bailey

Who defines beauty?

The question of ‘what is beauty has had a place in Western philosophy since the time of the Greek philosophers and to paraphrase Plato, ‘Beauty is in the eye of the beholder’.

In ‘day to day’ art and nature then yes, beauty can be said to be in the eye of the beholder, and this can be for a multitude of reasons.

Every individual will have a different predisposition of what is beautiful, their opinions and judgements on what is beautiful will be governed by past experiences, background thoughts, cultural identities and beliefs, so much so that the definition of beauty can only ever be subjective.

A mathematician can find beauty in algebra, an artist in a particular piece of artwork, a sportsman in the swing of Tiger Woods or the leap of Michael Jordan, an author in the prose of Thomas Hardy, a gardener in the smell, colour and feel of a rose in full bloom or a newly dug up carrot.

We can of course also have something that is beautiful and ugly at the same time.

‘Aesthetically ugly’ could be a word used to describe the English Carrier pigeon.

Its long neck and distinctive wattle have been developed over many years of selective breeding, to produce what during Victorian times was called ‘The King of Pigeons’.

Someone who had spent many years breeding these pigeons to produce a prize winning specimen, would certainly say that it was beautiful, whereas someone who had spent many years breeding an Indian fantail pigeon might argue that the carrier was ugly, whereas his fantail was undoubtedly beautiful.

We can argue at length over the merits of beauty, whether the person, animal, building, flower etc has order or symmetry or any other ‘guidelines’ for beauty, but there are times when perhaps the majority of us need to make an absolute decision on the ‘objective beauty’ of a certain thing or risk the chance of losing it.

If we introduce politics and money into the equation then at some stage wider society will have to come down hard and make a choice that asserts, for instance, that certain parts of a countries landscape are indeed beautiful and that property developers or logging and oil companies should not be allowed to build on, chop down or exploit.

If a property developer decides that a hotel and car park would look good next to Stonehenge, should we not be brave enough to stand up and argue that alongside it’s natural historical importance, it’s beauty lies in its location and the fields surrounding it and such a development should never be allowed.

The majority would also surely agree that the pristine and fragile arctic landscape and seas should not be sold off for oil exploration and drilling. In a last-ditch attempt to make good on promises to the oil and gas industry, the Trump administration is rushing to formalise plans to drill for oil in the Arctic national wildlife refuge before Joe Biden takes office.

What is beauty, is a philosophical question, that can never be answered, but can also be answered in a multitude of ways….. and at times HAS to be answered.
