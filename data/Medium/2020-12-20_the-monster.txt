The Monster

Tas Blogs

The voices echoed relentlessly hammering one after the other in time with the beating of my racing heart. Sweat poured down my neck and my palms grew clammy. I gulped, praying my lungs would expand to let in more oxygen or I swore I would drop dead in a faint. My hands were trembling as I squished and twisted my thumb cards attempting to release some of my nerves . I waited for my name to be called with the air of a person waiting to be shot.

Speaking infront of my class always induced the feelings of a burgeoning panic attack . I would stand up stumble my way to the front before mumbling my speech at lightning speed before the tears I was holding back began to fall. I barely waited for the feedback from the teacher to end before racing back and sinking into my seat in the corner. Gulping down water that was sloshing around in the bottle . I willed my heart to slow down and hands to stop shaking because I knew there was no reason for me to be feeling so petrified.

“It wasn’t that bad” my teacher commented looking at me through her broad lenses . Unknown to her, those words crushed me. It confirmed the belief that I was being ridiculous in my panic.

I knodded quietly keeping my gaze low. She wouldn’t understand just how much courage I had to forage for just to not cry infront of the girls I had known for five years.

I was a child when I realized that monsters didn’t live under the bed but rather they made their homes inside the heads of scared children .

The monster in my head had its claws sunken into the soft flesh of my heart and would squeeze warningly if I had to talk to a person I was not familiar with or groups bigger than two even if I was familiar with them.

There were days when I did try speak up in my group of classmates . The monster would still linger prowling in the back of my mind waiting to pounce . Whispering doubts and lies that spread and were internalized when my friends would look up and ask me if I had spoken and I would shake my head in a negative response. The monster won on those days.

It caused me to fade into a bystander in my own life watching as people forgot my existence due to my quietness. It hurt more than I liked to admit but then the hurt faded into resignation .

I still tried everyday to speak up. Pushing out the words despite inwardly wincing at my awkwardness. Eventually I did makr friends who were willing to be quiet with me and listen as I stuttered and stumbled my way through my story knodding and smiling encouragingly .

I hope one day that the monster would eventually fade into nothing but untill then I was willing to try everyday to not let it win.
