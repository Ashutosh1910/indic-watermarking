A tendril of smoke rose from the small blackened hole in the wall, a little flame burned and went out.

Mike sat still on the bed. He was in his red sleeping shorts; he had no shirt on. His feet pressed flat on the floor, and his hands were on the bedspread. The tall man across the room had undeniably captured his attention, but Mike’s wide eyes had moved to one side, and he was staring at the small smouldering hole that had just appeared in his bedroom wall.

“I see,” said Mike, and his eyes went slowly back to the man. “So it’s a gun then.”

The man bent over at the hips and held out the long object and looked down at it. He was taller than any person Mike had ever seen. His joints looked too far apart. He had on a black hat and sunglasses, and the skin of his face was white and… lumpy. Like the many layers of paint on a very old house. He had on a long grey coat. Probably wool. It looked good. Merino maybe. But it was far too big, and deep folds ran all the way down the coat like it was hung on hooks and there was nothing inside. The gloves were loose on the hands and the tips of the fingers were folded out from the fist. The man looked down at the long skinny object in his hands and said, “yes. You would call this a gun. Though I doubt you’ve ever seen one quite like it. ”

“No,” Mike said, slowly. “Dainty, isn’t it?” A silent beat passed between them and the tall man stood up straight again. And Mike said, “So, then — let me get this straight. You are coming with me.”

“No,” said the man. “I am be-com-ing you. I will — borrow — your body. We will become one.”

“Ah,” said Mike and his mouth stayed open. He moved his head back in a nod that he didn’t complete. “I see.”

“Fantastic,” said the man.

“And you need me to eat?” said Mike.

“That’s right.”

“But me no hungy,” said Mike.

“Oh,” said the man. “But you will be.”

So they went together down the stairs, and already the kitchen smelled delicious. The table was set and dinner was served. Steak, peas and carrots, and a dollop of mashed potatoes. When was Mike was done eating he took the plate to the kitchen sink and he rinsed it in the cold water, and he placed the plate and the cutlery into the sink and washed his hands.

“That was delicious,” said Mike. His hands were wet, he looked around.

“Are you ready?” said the tall man. He was seated at the table, and he faced the darkened staircase.

“I reckon,” said Mike. And he reached into the pantry cupboard and the handle of the bat was leaning on the doorframe. “But first, let me show you how to mash potatoes.”
