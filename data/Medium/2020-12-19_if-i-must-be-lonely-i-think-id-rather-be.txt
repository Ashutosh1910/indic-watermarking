“If I must be lonely I think I’d rather be alone” — Stabbing Westward

unknown

She sat at the cafe table, her pen tapping lightly on her ceramic mug. She bounced her leg up and down, trying to focus on the words circling around her mind. A small frustrated huff escapes her mouth as once again, she can’t grab the right words. A menacing blank page sat in front of her, waiting for her to fill it. One look at her now and you would have never thought she was a poet. She only seemed to trip and fall over precariously placed words. Each letter didn’t fit in the endless puzzle and her mind couldn’t get rid of the knots tangled in the strings of her thoughts.

As the young woman sat back in her chair, she looked up at the rest of the world. She watched as business folk walked in brisk strides and shop owner’s shouted out prices for their products. She watched as the children laughed and giggled, tugged their mother’s and father’s along, begging for the fruits or jewelry. She saw beggars tucked under small awnings supplied by the storefronts, holding their hands out to those who are generous enough to give. She watched as the world seemed to pass her by. And that’s when it hit her.

It wasn’t intended when words started to pour on the pages, scribbles turning into words and words turning into sentences. She hadn’t meant to pour her heart out to those who she could never meet. She never planned to be laid so bare, and yet feel so heard. In that moment, all she could do was let the dam break and hope that her emotions made it onto the page. She hoped that one day these words that filled these notebooks could do something.

Eventually the entire page was full and she was forced to stop and take a step back. As she dragged in a deep breath, she began to read the words on the page. Her voice stuttered at every pause and her breaths were ragged. Some of the words flowed like liquid gold and others had a roughness of steel, but in due time, she felt that it would make sense. Waiting a few heartbeats, she gathered herself and began to change it.

She crossed out the words she didn’t like and replaced them with ones that felt just right. She made sure to speak clearly when reading it because if said wrong, it would lose every bit of its flow. She precariously held a thesaurus on her lap as she turned the fringed and creased papers, searching and searching until she found what she had been looking for. Piece by piece that poem made a bit more sense, and in the end, the puzzle was finally solved.

As she reflected on her work, tears began to track down her face. Finally understanding the words on the page, she saw herself, reflecting back at her. In the end, she never ended up writing for anyone else but herself. But people just always seemed to feel the way she felt. And feeling lonely doesn’t mean you’re alone because maybe everyone else in the world was just a bit lonely too.
