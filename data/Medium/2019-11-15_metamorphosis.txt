It was in the dead of night that I awoke in a bed not my own, next to a man I did not know.

I was at once afraid, but not for this alone. It was the shadow that stood hunched in the corner of the room that stole my attention, reducing me to a bed of tears and instilling in me the fear of my apparent mortality. Each passing moment brought with it a new stage of metamorphosis as the shadow gradually took form, presenting before my eyes a stature for which the room could hardly contain, yet this appeared to be a mere trifle. These walls would accommodate it, its presence was thus; a solid mass which, simultaneously, filled the room like smoke.

A moment passed, and it’s trunk like arms hung from the ceiling to the floor. Another, and it’s perfectly round head seemed close to falling, surely to be followed by the rest of it. Then, in the blink of an eye, jutting forth from its head were two crimson marbles that glistened amidst the darkness. They defied logic — a singularity for which I had no explanation. But I suppose we were well past that stage, for there was no sense to be made about the figure that stood before me.

All transformations ceased as I feared for what was to come next, yet nothing happened. It simply stood there — watching; waiting. For what, I wondered, but was too afraid to pose such questions on it. I closed my eyes and thought of you, of how I wished you would save me from this terrible nightmare. A small voice inside me called out to you, to which my tormentor answered with encroaching steps. It stood motionless at the foot of the bed. My eyes refused to open, but I knew — I could feel it.

“You ought to be ashamed of yourself,” it said, in a husky voice that sounded strangely familiar. Curious, I opened my eyes to see your brother standing there. Resting naturally atop his head was a wig that did not fit his image: A pearlescent bowl-cut on the head of a tall, stocky black male. This had to be a dream, this could not possibly be real. I found it amusing; this was just the sort of thing my subconscious would conjure up.

I asked him what he was doing here, but he would not answer. I asked if he recognised the man beside me; he responded with continued silence.

“What was that baby?” said the man laying beside me, as he awoke.

It was then that I realised it was happening again. As if leaving him wasn’t enough, he continued to haunt me in my dreams. I attempted to leap from the bed, but found that I could not.

“You ought to be ashamed of yourself,” said your brother, with added scorn in his voice. I pleaded for his help, but the words would not reach my lips. He looked at me with unfeeling eyes and shrugged before descending through the wooden floorboards, to be seen no more. He left me paralysed in this strange room, bedraggled in sweat, and marred by a guilty conscience. Alone, with only the man from whom I have long since tried to escape from to comfort me; promising that I would be safe.

My tormentor had taken its final form — its metamorphosis was complete.
