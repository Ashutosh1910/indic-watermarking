Through The Looking Glass

Melissa R. Mendelson

NYC Midnight Short Story

“Help me! My cat’s not responding, and she is not going to make it.”

“Mrs. Whisper, calm down.” The veterinarian appeared behind her. “Let me take her from you.”

Mrs. Whisper handed the cat to him. “She’s still breathing. I can’t lose her. I just lost my husband last year.”

The veterinarian placed a hand on her shoulder. “Breathe,” he said. “I’m going to take her into the exam room.”

“Okay.” She wiped her tears away. “Will you be euth….”

“Euthanizing her? I don’t think I need to.” The veterinarian walked into the exam room and placed the cat on a small table. He reached for a box of tissues, handing them to Mrs. Whisper.

“Thank you,” and Mrs. Whisper blew her nose. She watched the veterinarian open a drawer, expecting him to pull out a syringe. He took out a magnifying glass and handed it to her.

“I’m going to leave the room, but I’m dimming the lights first. When you’re alone, hold the magnifying glass over the cat. What’s your cat’s name?”

“Jelly Bean. What does the magnifying glass do? Can’t you see that my cat is dying?”

“She is, and I want to give you a chance to say good-bye.” The veterinarian dimmed the lights and left the room.

Mrs. Whisper looked at the cat. “I’m not going anywhere,” and she held the magnifying glass over the cat.

Dim light trickled down from the ceiling. It floated like a million little golden feathers, and the cat’s body began to hum. Her limbs turned into legs and arms, and her face morphed from cat to human. An older woman sat up on the small table.

Mrs. Whisper nearly dropped the magnifying glass, but her arm was frozen in the air. “What’s wrong with my arm? Am I having a stroke?”

“No, Mary. You’re not,” the woman said. “Could I have that blanket you are holding?” Mrs. Whisper gave her the blanket. “Thank you,” and she wrapped the blanket around herself.

“Who are you?”

“Jelly Bean.”

“No, Jelly Bean is a cat.”

“It’s easier this way. You’ve been through so much, Mary, and we had our own ups and downs. I forgive you.” Mrs. Whisper cried, and Jelly Bean held her as she cried.

“I can’t lose you,” Mrs. Whisper howled. “I’ll be alone.”

“Call your daughters,” Jelly Bean said. “I don’t want you to be alone.”

“I love you.”

“I love you too.” The older woman turned back into a cat. A moment later, she was gone.

The veterinarian stepped into the room. He took the magnifying glass from Mrs. Whisper and placed it back in the drawer. He took the blanket and covered Jelly Bean. “I’m sorry,” he said.

“Thank you. How much do I owe you?”

“Nothing. When the ashes come back, I’ll call you. Go home. Call your daughters.”

“I’m going to miss her.”

“When you see your reflection, take a closer look, and she’ll be there.” The veterinarian watched Mrs. Whisper leave the room.
