Dematerialized

Melissa R. Mendelson

A short story

“Do you have to burn that one?”

Josh looked at the book in his hand. All the magazines were already burnt. The hard covers barely burned. The paperbacks would keep them warm. He ran his finger along the cover. “Our hearts are not in Atlantis,” he said. “They’re already gone,” and he threw the book into the fire.

“That was my favorite,” Taylor said. She brushed a few tears aside. She picked up a mug and took a sip. Her head jerked back. “This is disgusting,” and she placed the mug down near the fireplace.

“It’s cold chicken noodle soup.” Josh lifted his mug and forced himself to drink the broth. He chewed on a piece of chicken and placed the mug back down on the floor.

“We’re going to get sick,” Taylor said. “Probably have to go to the hospital, and you know what will happen there.”

Josh lifted the mug up to his lips, but he couldn’t take another sip. “No more soup. Not until the power is back, and the heat. At least, the toilet works.”

“I can’t live like this, Josh. I just can’t.”

“That’s why we sent the kids to live with your parents.” Josh pulled Taylor close. “Come closer to the fire, please.”

“And watch our books burn? No, I can’t look at the fire.”

“Then, look at me.” Josh held her stare. “We will get through this.”

“How? There’s no money coming in. We’re nearly out of food. We just have a cabinet filled with cans of soup that we can’t even cook. We couldn’t even feed the dog.” A sob caught in her throat.

“Do the kids know?”

“No. I couldn’t tell them. Maybe, instead of paying both monthly bills on our cell phones, you should just cover yours.”

“Taylor…”

“No, Josh. We need to eat and not make ourselves sick. We have to stay away from the hospital.”

“Okay. It would give us a little extra.”

“I could go for pizza.” Taylor watched Josh laugh. “What?”

“Nothing.” Josh wrapped his arm around her. “I love you.”

“I love you too. This whole thing is a nightmare. So many people are suffering. So many are dead.”

“There’s a silver lining.”

“What?”

“Not that people are suffering or dead. I mean that there is a silver lining because we can’t watch the news.” Josh glanced at the television set behind them. “The news forces you to watch all that suffering and death, and it just destroys you.” Josh watched the last of the book burn. “When did we lose ourselves?”

“A long time ago. Would it be wrong to hope that I fall asleep and not wake up?”

“No.” Josh kissed her cheek. “But we need to survive, and when this is over, we have to make sure that it never happens again.”

“Let’s just get through the night, and then worry about tomorrow.” Kayla rested her head against Josh’s shoulder, and they sat together, watching the fire consume pieces of their lives.
