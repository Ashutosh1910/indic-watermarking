I MIGHT NEED A GUN FOR THE NEXT TIME I HEAR SUCH STATEMENTS!

unknown

“Ebu jifunze kupika ama ng’ombe zitapungua!”

“Wewe ni mchafu huwezi pata bwana!”

“Mtakula nini na bwana kama hujui kupika?”

I’m sure you have been told such statements more often than not if you are a girl and have grown in an African home, Kenyan to be specific. Lightly translated that your value as a woman is lowered if you are not good at certain household skills and marriage will be difficult for you. Such comments make young girls spend most of their childhood preparing themselves for marriage, learning stuff not for themselves but because someday they are going to be someone’s wife or mother. We have made them believe marriage is the goal, a lifetime achievement, the end game.

Recently I’ve been wondering why women feel the need to get married at a certain age or feel like failures when they don’t get married at a certain time or age bracket. Then it hit me that all this stems from how we raise our girls, the expectations set on them from a young age. Constantly encouraging them to learn basic life skills such as cooking, cleaning, washing, personal hygiene as a set up for marriage and not just as essential basic skills everyone should learn. From a young age, through childhood, teenage hood and adult hood we constantly groom women for marriage.

So what happens if I don’t get married at a certain age? Or I don’t get married at all? Or I get married and it doesn’t go as planned? Of course I’ll see myself as a failure, I’ll carry the burden of shame and pressure set on me by society. I have been preparing for this thing all my life, I’ve been groomed for it, learnt everything they said I should learn and now I’m at that age (not that there’s one but again societal pressure) but still I’ve not found this long term partner. Of course I’ll see myself as a failure!

This is the sickening messed up mentality we raise our girls with. I believe everyone should learn such basic skills entirely independent of their gender, not as a set up for marriage or motherhood but because they are essentials in life just as skills such as communication and teamwork are. The sad part about all this is that our male counterparts never go through the same, we often hear “let boys be boys”. Talk about double standards that should remain in the past. I’m surprised they still exist.

What we don’t see is that we are hurting a generation of beautiful women, strong women, resilient women, go-getters and bosses. Making them seem as failures, placing unnecessary pressure on them just because of this one thing, marriage. Marriage is beautiful but it’s not the end game, it’s not the only goal and not a lifetime achievement. We can do better by our girls for sure, teach them that they are whole with or without marriage. Don’t rush it, the timelines are just an illusion and societal standards are a constant moving target, a few years back it was trendy to shave your eyebrows and now we are drawing them back. Sis you can’t keep aiming at a moving target, move at your pace.
