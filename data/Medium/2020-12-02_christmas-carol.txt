Christmas Carol

Matthew Donnellon

Names are a funny thing.

Sometimes you see someone and their name just fits like a fine leather glove slipped over a hand.

That person is the name.

And that name is the person.

Sometimes though, things are different.

This is the story of a woman named Carol and a cruel, cruel world.

Carol grew up like most kids.

Her parents decided to name her after the girl’s deceased grandmother.

And so young Carol began life like many others. She had by all accounts a happy childhood, with two loving parents.

He spent her days with her dad and they both greeted mom when she got home from work.

It was generally great all around.

Like many young children, Carol had a special affinity for Christmas. It is always more magical through the eyes of a child.

Each year Carol ran to the living room to see if there were presents. And lucky enough there always were.

One year she got a doll.

The next a fire truck. It lit up and everything.

Despite having it pretty good, Carol finally reached the age where she started going to school.

She liked school well enough, but like most children would have preferred to stay home.

She did well in school. She made friends. Things were going well.

Until her first December.

Christmas was coming and usually this meant good things for Carol. But now she was in school and had to deal with other children. And sometimes children are cruel.

Like many others, Carol picked up a nickname during this time of the year.

All the little kids knew it, “Christmas Carol. Christmas Carol. Christmas Carol,” they all sang.

It followed her through school.

Each year around Christmas time the little chants would start.

And it went on and on.

It followed her to college when she let one of her friends know about the nickname. The woman told all the people in the dorm, and so the chorus began anew.

“Christmas Carol,” the all said.

Carol hated it.

Hated them.

It drove her mad. She wished they’d all just go away.

But the new year came and people forgot about it and returned to the alcohol fueled stupor that is college life.

Carol thought she was safe. For nearly a year it went away. But the nickname returned at Christmas time like a ghost determined to haunt her favorite holiday.

Carol graduated a year early just to make sure she didn’t have to hear it again.

She took a job on the other side of the state where no one knew her, and didn’t answer any friend requests from her old life.

She had a good job at a nice firm.

She did well and was going to get promoted when the new year came,

Then it happened.

It was the firm’s Christmas party.

She heard again as her boss made a joke that Christmas Carol would be the new manager.

She ran to an abandoned room.

She fought back tears.

No.

No.

No.

It couldn’t happen again.

She wouldn’t let it.

Carol realized she was in the janitor’s closet and found a bottle on the floor.

When she came out she spied the punch bowl.

She would never hear that again.

No more Christmas Carol.

This time they would all go away.
