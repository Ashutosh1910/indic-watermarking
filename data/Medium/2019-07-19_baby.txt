Baby

Tracy Aston

Flash Fiction

I can hear my parents talking about names. I listen to them whenever I’m not asleep, well, I have no choice. They’re excited about my arrival and they’ve decorated a room especially for me. They’ve done it out in green. It’s not because they don’t know I’m a boy; they don’t believe in blue for a boy and pink for a girl. And they’ve bought clothes and toys and a wooden cot and a mobile of coloured animals to hang above it. But I won’t need any of those things, not even a name, because I’m not coming out.

She has noticed her stomach seems to be getting smaller. We are at the doctor’s. He’s sending her for a scan. Now she’s ringing the man who thinks he’s my father. I can just hear him saying he’s in a meeting. She starts to cry. He says he’ll meet her at the hospital as soon as he can.

If I come out he will look at me and say he wants me tested and she will break down and tell him I am not his. Then he will leave her. She will grow to resent me and that resentment will turn to hate. By the age of ten I will be out of control and skipping school. She will be called in by the teachers and will say it’s my fault she has to take tablets. And when she has a nervous breakdown they will put me in a home. I will keep running away until they put me in a foster home where there is a boy and a girl.

They will blame me for everything they do wrong and each time the parents will believe them and the father will hit me with his belt and I will ask to go back to the home. But when I run away again they won’t find me.

When I am fourteen I will be on the street, living on food I’ll steal from the barrows at the market. I will get hassled by men and I will sleep under the bridge where no one comes, except women, brought there by businessmen in their company cars.

When I am sixteen I will meet a girl called Stacey who I will really like but won’t sleep with in case I make a child I can’t take care of. I will be caught breaking into a shop and be sent to a detention centre where I will cut myself. A psychologist will come and talk to me and I will try to say what I think he wants to hear so he will let me out. But he won’t, and I will stop speaking and I will keep cutting myself. Then they will watch me to make sure I don’t kill myself. They will say I’m depressed and give me tablets which I won’t take because they remind me of my mother. Then when I am let out, I will climb to the top of a block of flats and throw myself off.

The doctors can’t explain why I am slowly disappearing. My parents get angry with them then cry. I am getting smaller every day. I hope it won’t be long before I can no longer hear them crying. But their marriage will grow stronger because of the loss of me. He won’t leave her again and she won’t get pregnant by a man she turns to while he’s gone.

They won’t risk trying to have another child but will adopt a boy, and they will be happy for most of their time. And the boy will grow up knowing he is loved.
