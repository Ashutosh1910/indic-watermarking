Apocalypse Now and Again

Robert Turner

Based on real events in the heart of Asia

We are desperately looking for cover. We’ve radioed in our position and they’ve scrambled the fighters. In minutes napalm will rain from the skies and our enemy will be incinerated.

Their tiny lungs will explode from the searing heat and we will sit and observe with gleeful smiles from the safety of cover as they writhe on the floor in agony.

Okay, so no planes were coming, but the thought was immensely satisfying. I pick another very tiny red fire ant off my foot and crush it with relish. I am an animal and nature lover no more, this is war. There will be no prisoners, screw the Geneva Convention.

I only become aware of my tiny little passenger after he has sunken his pincers into my already swollen foot. The agonizing bite alerts me to the other eight little red ninjas, that have climbed unnoticed onto my feet and are now trying to select a virgin section of foot to set ablaze.

They are silent, deadly and completely immune to water, fire, motor oil and a selection of other liquids and solids we unleash on them. It’s their garden and no way are they giving it up without a fight. Game on. Our enemy may be small, but they are legion.

The problem is monumental. They outnumber us by millions, they have dug an intricate network of underground bunkers and they possess state of the art communication systems and lookout sentries posted in the trees with binoculars.

Don’t believe me? Leave a piece of food unattended in any location, and I do mean anywhere, for longer than five seconds and it’s gone, carried off by the enemy's supply lines which magically appear from nowhere.

Remain stationary for longer than ten seconds and the suicide squads strike. With no regard for their own safety, they attack remorselessly, resulting in the unfortunate victim having to perform a spontaneous rain dance to try and dislodge them. All to no avail. They’ve dug in and surgical tools are required to prise them loose.

It’s not a fair fight. We are bigger, supposedly brighter and definitely stronger, so why am I staring at my swollen feet again, on the verge of frustrated tears, wondering where it all went so wrong.

Whilst pondering this question, I am forced into the dance again, having stood still for longer than ten seconds to think. Stupid human. Forget the napalm, it’s time to go nuclear.

It’s just started to rain and an evil smile crosses my lips as I see tiny red butts scuttling for cover. The smile doesn't last long though, a sharp bite on the shoulder reminding me I forgot the mosquito repellant.

I wonder what the neighbors would think if they saw the foreigner gardening in a wetsuit?

I am also starting to suspect that the ants may be behind the slogan, ‘It’s more fun in the Philippines’. What better way to lure in a food source.
