Anticipating The Movie Onward And Processing Grief

Priya Sridhar

I dreamed about my dad when he died in 2001. The first time, I woke up thinking he was alive and being sad when I realized he wasn’t. I cried in my dream the second time, knowing he wasn’t real. If there was a third time, I couldn’t tell you. I would have used a spell to talk to him for one last time.

Animated films about grief are quite interesting. Some films tell us there is no magic solution, and go into the grieving process quite thoroughly. Inside Out did great with that premise. Others gloss over it for the sake of the story, like when dealing with the king and queen’s deaths with frozen. The Lion King was remarkable for showing how grief follows you over the years and doesn’t fade unless you confront it head-on and make peace with your pain.

Onward, a Pixar movie premiering on March 6, is more of the latter. We can at least hope for a film that shows the process of grief, and how we live with it. Pixar knows how to tackle sadness and everything else that comes with those terrible feelings.

The film is about two elf brothers who go on a quest to reunite with their father for 24 hours. It’s not a permanent revival. So far they only have his legs. The brothers want the rest of him, to have a proper goodbye. It’s also a world where magic is dying, and they have to find it again to get that perfect moment. Tom Holland plays the lead, Ian Lightfoot. I originally wrote this draft assuming it was Tom Hiddleston, but no it’s the other Tom. Even so, they’re all still Marvel stars.

Sadness is not going to dominate the premise, however; we are also going to see suburban fantasy with magical creatures in human jobs. There are manticores, centaurs, and pixies filling typical roles. They serve as officers of the law or riding bikes. We won’t be seeing a story where the creatures have to hide behind a masquerade. They coexist in a semi-functioning society.

The movie may be good. It certainly hits close to home since I know I won’t ever see my dad again in that sense. What’s more, it strikes to avoid the dreaded sequel or merchandise trap that seemed to plague Cars and Toy Story. We love the merch, but we also want Disney to earn our love. They did that with Inside Out, after all.

The big question becomes the following: will Onward usher in a new era of Pixar? Can they erase past scandals or mistakes? Will we see a comeback?

I hope so. Pixar is also coming out with a movie called Soul, about an aspiring musician that gets caught in a divine mixup. It’s been a while since I’ve expressed interest in a film and I want Onward to be the film that makes me fall in love with Pixar again. Maybe this will be the one, that will make me think of my dad, and what I would say to him.
