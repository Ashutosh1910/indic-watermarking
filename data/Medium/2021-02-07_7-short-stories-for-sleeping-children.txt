The Princess and the Pea

The Princess and the Pea

He was once a prince and that prince wanted to marry a princess, but a real princess. And he went about to find one that pleased him, and found none. There were enough princesses, but he couldn’t tell if they were really princesses, because everyone was missing something. And the prince returned home again and was very upset, because he really wanted to find a real princess.

One evening a terrible storm had just arisen, thunder and lightning, and it was raining buckets; it was awful weather. Suddenly there was a knock at the city gate and the king, the prince’s father, went to open it.

There was a princess at the gate. Alas, but in what condition it was because of the rain and bad weather! Water trickled down his hair and clothes and he slipped into his shoes at the toes and dripped back to his heels. But she said she was a real princess.

“Okay, I’ll see you right away!” the queen thought, but said nothing; he went to the bedroom, took all the mattresses from the bed, and placed a pea on the boards of the bed. Then he placed twenty woollen mattresses over the peas and twenty large down pillows on the mattresses.

This is where the princess slept. In the morning they asked her how she slept.

- Terribly stupid! replied the princess. I didn’t close my eyes all night. God knows what was in bed! I sat on something hard and now it’s all just bruises! Terrible!

And then they all saw that she was a real princess, if she felt a pea through twenty mattresses and twenty down pillows. Only a princess could be so gentle on her skin!

Star rain

Star rain

She was once a little girl whose mother and father had died, and she was so poor that she had no room to live in, no bed to sleep in, nothing but her clothes and the piece of bread she had. some merciful passers-by gave it to her from time to time.

But she was a good and faithful little girl. And, as she was abandoned by everyone, she left, with God in mind, in the field, at random. At one point he met a poor man who said to him:

- Give me some food, I’m starving. She handed him the last piece of bread he had with her, then said, “God bless you,” and left.

On the way he met a child who said to him:

- My head is really cold, give me something to cover. The little girl didn’t think long, took her hat off and handed it to him.

And again a naked child appeared, shivering with cold. She handed him her coat and left. He arrived in a forest in the evening; a poor woman came to her and the little girl gave her the shirt she still had, thinking: “It’s night, nobody sees me, I can stay naked.”

And, as she stood, she suddenly saw a rain of stars above her head, which, when they fell to the ground, turned into gold coins. A gold-threaded tunic and a precious ring fell from the sky. She put on her tunic, put her ring on her finger, then began to gather the gold coins; she thus became very rich and then lived in abundance and happiness all her life.

The rabbit and the snail

You have them all beautiful, hedgehog, only your legs are crooked and they are a bit tangled.

The hedgehog is upset:

You laugh in vain; my crooked legs are quicker than yours straight. Let’s just go home for a moment and then compete!

We had a misunderstanding with the rabbit and we want to compete to see which of us runs faster.

“You’re out of your mind!” the hedgehog told him. How are you going to compete with the rabbit? He’s quick on his foot, and your paws are crooked and blunt.

“He may be quick-footed, but I’m quicker-minded,” said the hedgehog. And you do what I tell you. Come to the field!

They arrived on the plow; the hedgehog said to his wife:

How long have I been waiting for you!

The three piglets

The three piglets

Once upon a time there was a piglet, which had 3 piglets, but did not have enough food for all three. So, when they were old enough, their mother sent them into the world to try their luck.

The first piglet was very lazy. He didn’t want to work at all and built a thatched house.

The second piglet was a little more industrious, but he had his moments of laziness. He built a house out of sticks.

The third piglet was the hardest working. He worked all day and built a solid brick house.

The next day, a wolf passed through the forest where the three piglets had built their houses. He saw the straw house, smelled the first piglet, and was hungry.

So he knocked on the door and asked the piglet to let him in:

Piglet, piglet, let me in!

But the piglet saw the wolf’s big paws and realized his intentions:

“Go away, wolf!” I won’t let you in!

Hearing this, the wolf bared his teeth and said:

“Then I’ll swell and blow, so I’ll put your house down!”

The wolf took a deep breath and tore down the piglet’s house. He opened his mouth as wide as he could and grabbed the piglet, but he escaped and managed to reach his brother, the one with the house in the sticks.

The wolf continued to walk through the forest until he reached the front of the house from the sticks. Here he smelled the two piglets, and his hunger hit him even harder. He knocked on the door again:

“Little pigs, little pigs, let me in!”

Looking through the keyhole, the piglets saw the sharp ears of the wolf and realized the danger:

“Go away, wolf!” We won’t let you in!

Hearing this, the wolf bared his teeth and said:

“Then I’ll swell and blow, I’ll knock your house down!”

The wolf took a deep breath, swelled and blew, tearing down the piglets’ straw house. Out of his greed, the wolf tried to catch both pigs, so they both escaped.

The wolf ran them through the forest and almost caught them, but they managed to hide in the brick house of their brother, the third piglet.

The three piglets were very scared, knowing that the wolf wanted to eat them. They were right to be scared, because the wolf, after running through the forest, was even hungrier than before.

So he knocked on the door of the third piglet:

“Little pigs, little pigs, let me in!”

Looking through the keyhole, the piglets saw the wicked eyes of the wolf and realized the danger:

“Go away, wolf!” We won’t let you in!

Hearing this, the wolf bared his teeth and said:

“Then I’ll swell and blow, I’ll knock your house down!”

The wolf swelled and blew once, twice, three times, but in vain. Seeing that he could not knock down the third house, he angrily decided to enter the chimney.

Cleverly, however, the third piglet quickly made a fire in the fireplace, and when the wolf landed it roasted in its tail.

Sensing the burning sting, the wolf fled eating the earth on the chimney back and disappeared into the woods.

Thus, the three piglets escaped unharmed and learned an important lesson.

Finger

Finger

A long time ago, in a distant land, an old woman was sitting in her seesaw and thinking about how much joy a child would bring her.

Then he heard a knock on the door and went to open it. A woman stood in the doorway and said, “If you let me sleep here at night, I will grant you a wish.”

The old woman opened them because she felt sorry for the woman and because she knew exactly what she wanted to do: a child.

He fed the guest and let her take a bath, then realized that she was very beautiful.

The woman slept well overnight, and when she awoke, before leaving, she asked the hostess: — Well, what wish do you want me to fulfill?

The woman remembered how most of those she met wanted wealth, strength, and beauty. And when he heard the woman’s wish, he couldn’t believe it.

I’d like to have a baby.

The woman put a small seed in the old woman’s hand and said:

Plant this seed, water it carefully, watch over it and love it with all your heart. If you do all this, you will have a child.

The old woman listened to his advice with holiness. A week later, instead of the seed, there was a yellow flower. The next day the flower opened, and out of the flower came a little girl the size of the old woman’s thumb.

Thus, the woman called her Finger.

The old woman sewed a small dress with golden threads and prepared her bed in a walnut shell. They were both happy and enjoying each other’s presence.

One day, when Little Finger went to bed, a frog jumped into her room on the window and said, “You will be the perfect bride for my son.”

The frog grabbed Thumbelina and took her to a water lily leaf, then went to look for her son.

The little finger began to cry so loudly that the fish in the lake heard it and began to gnaw at the root of the water lily, so that it could float on the lake.

Fingers floated on the water lily leaf for hours, until it reached the shore. In summer, it fed on berries and leaf dew.

But the cold winter came and she had to find shelter. A friendly mouse let her stay with him, but explained that he could not host her too long and that he would have to marry a friend of the mouse.

Leaving to meet her future husband, Degețica ended up in a tunnel where she found a bird that seemed breathless. The girl felt sorry for her and wanted to bury her, but she realized that the bird was just hurt.

He cared for her until, when spring came, the bird fully recovered and flew into the air.

Spring and summer passed, and Finger, although she did not want to, was preparing for the wedding with her future husband, the mouse. Just before the wedding, she heard a familiar chirp: it was the bird she had healed:

“You can come with me to warm countries,” the bird told him.

The little finger jumped on the back of the bird and flew to the warm countries. The people there, who were as small as her, named her Erin. There Erin met a prince and the two lived happily ever after.

The story of the bunny Peter

The story of the bunny Peter

There were once four bunnies, named Impiedicilă, Ghemotoc, Codiţa-de-cotton and Peter. They lived with their mother in a sandy bed below a very large fir tree.

“My dears,” said Mrs. Rabbit one morning, “you are allowed to play on the plain or on the path, but never in Mr. McGregor’s garden. Your father had an accident there, and was then put in a pie by Mrs. Mc Gregor. ”

“Now you can go out, but be careful, without the impudence! I have to go.” Then Mrs. Rabbit took her basket and umbrella, and went through the woods to the bakery, whence she bought a black loaf and five raisin buns.

Impedicila, Ghemotoc, and Cotton-Tail were good rabbits, so they went on the path to pick blackberries.

But Peter, who was very naughty, ran straight into Mr. McGregor’s garden, sneaking under the gate.

First, he ate some salad and some French beans; then he swallowed some radishes. And finally, feeling a little unwell, he looked for some parsley. But just as he reached the end of the cucumber grinder, he met Mr. McGregor!

Mr. McGregor stood on all fours planting some tender cabbage, but immediately jumped to his feet and ran after Peter, shaking a rake and shouting, “Stop, thief!” Peter lost one of his shoes among the cabbages, and dropped the other in the middle of the potatoes.

After running out of shoes, he ran in four paws as fast as he could to get rid of the pursuer, but, unfortunately, he found himself in a tangle of gooseberries, clinging to them with the big buttons of his coat. It was a blue coat with brass buttons, which he had recently received as a gift.

Peter gave up, and began to weep with crocodile tears; his sighs were heard by some friendly swallows, who flew swiftly at him, and begged him to try to get out of there. Mr. McGregor was sure Peter was peeing somewhere in the shed, maybe even under one of the flowerpots, so he began to pick them up carefully, one by one, looking carefully under each one.

Suddenly, Peter sneezed, “Hapciu!” Mr. McGregor rushed at him without thinking. The owner of the garden tried to put Peter’s foot to catch him, but the bunny jumped out of the shed window, thus breaking three flower pots. The window was too small for Mr. McGregor, and he was too tired to run after Peter, so he went back to his business.

Peter took a break to rest. The bunny then tried to cross the garden, but was even more confused, as he came near a small pool, where Mr. McGregor was filling his bucket with water. Peter turned to the shed but suddenly heard, very close to him, some noises sounding something like, “Hâr, hârr, hârrr.” The bunny took shelter under a bush and, seeing that nothing was happening to him, climbed on a wheelbarrow and looked around. He was seen by Mr. McGregor digging in the ground to plant some onions. He turned his back on him, and a little farther on he could see the garden gate!

Peter got off the wheelbarrow as slowly as he could and began to run as fast as he could along a straight path behind some currant bushes.

Mr. McGregor saw him just as he reached the corner of this path, but Peter didn’t care anymore. He snuck under the gate and reached the forest safely. Peter did not stop running and did not look back until he reached his house under the tree.

He was so tired that he stumbled on the fine sandy carpet at the entrance to his nest, and his eyes closed. His mother was busy cooking, but he wondered what Peter had done with his clothes. It was the second time in two weeks that the bunny had lost his coat and shoes.

I’m sorry to tell you, Peter wasn’t feeling well that night. His mother took him to bed, made some chamomile tea, and, moreover, gave Peter a portion! She told her naughty son, “You have to take a spoonful each time before you fall asleep.”

His brothers, Impiedicilă, Ghemotoc, and Codiţă-de-bumbac had a tasty black bread, raisin buns, milk and blackberries that evening!
