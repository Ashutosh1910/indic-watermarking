We Need More Capable Female Characters Like Olivia Benson

Rebeca Ansar

Law & Order: Special Victims Unit (or SVU) is a police procedural television series created by Dick Wolf. Since 1999, the show has been successfully running on NBC for 21 seasons. The show first featured detective duo Olivia Benson, played by Mariska Hargitay, and Elliot Stabler, played by Christopher Meloni.

Meloni did not return for the show’s 13th season, and Hargitay’s character began to move increasingly to the forefront of the show. In the past seven seasons, Olivia Benson has moved up in rank from Detective to Sergeant to Lieutenant. Presently she is Captain.

Mariska Hargitay has shown unyielding dedication to carrying out the role of Olivia Benson. She is “the only original cast member still present on the series,” and “is the longest-serving character of all time in the Law & Order universe.”

There is some healing that happens even though the character is fictional. Just knowing that hearts like Benson’s are possible gives me hope for the future.

Even though each show’s storyline is formulaic, Hargitay’s emotional and intellectual ranges when portraying Benson continue to draw viewership.

For those who say that female leads aren’t able to competitively appeal to an audience, Olivia Benson is an example demonstrating that the rest of the entertainment industry just isn’t trying hard enough. Season 21 of SVU has marked it as the “longest-running primetime live-action series.” Olivia Benson “is the longest-running female character” in that genre.

There’s a reason why. This character is an embodiment of traits for which our culture is desperate.

When Olivia Benson talks to victims of assault, she believes them. That’s her first job. She listens to their stories, no matter how complicated or half-missing they may be. She follows up. She does her due diligence. She puts herself in the trenches with victims to as much of a degree as she can.

Often, she goes above and beyond, helping the viewers understand that this kind of dedication is necessary to end a culture of assault and rape.

The character’s personal life is also intricately laced through the show’s seasons. In it, we are given pieces of who Benson is as a person. While she does have romantic interests, her job is her priority. She makes this very clear. Hers is not a character that exists in relation to a man.

While many of us have been conditioned to believe the tired trope that women like this are cold and unreachable, Benson is anything but an ice queen. She’s a full-fledged human with a complete emotional palette and enough empathy and willpower for ten people.

This is arguably most notable in the child she chooses to adopt. Noah Porter Benson is the biological son of a drug addict mother and pimp and serial rapist father. He becomes orphaned. Through a brutally arduous process, Oliva adopts him and learns to mother him with empathy and understanding. For example, in a recent episode, Noah expresses his wish to trade in his baseball glove for ballet slippers. She enrolls her son in dance class by the end of the episode.
