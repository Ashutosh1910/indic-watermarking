He Played Snots in National Lampoon’s Christmas Vacation and It Ruined His Life

The role of a lifetime was a ticket to typecasting hell and a struggle for respect in Hollywood.

It’s been over 30 years since Alonzo Contreras played Snots in the hit 1989 movie National Lampoon’s Christmas Vacation.

While Contreras said he’s honored “people love something that I was a part of,” the actor wants people to stop asking him to “do the snotting thing” for them.

“I get asked that every day I leave the house,” Contreras shared with Access Entertainment. “What am I supposed to say? They don’t understand that it was all special effects. I’m like, ‘I don’t have a sinus condition, I don’t know what to tell you.’ ”

“I never want to take away their enjoyment of the moment,” he said. “But … I’m not a performing monkey. I’m more than that one character.”

The Role of a Lifetime

According to Contreras, portraying Snots in the movie, about the Griswold family’s plans for a big family Christmas that turns into a disaster, was a “blessing and curse.” The role shot Contreras to stardom while simultaneously typecasting the actor for decades.

“I got into acting after I saw the Bumpus’ hounds in A Christmas Story,” Contreras shared. “When I saw them demolish that kitchen I was transported. I knew that’s what I wanted to do with my life.”

“Before Christmas Vacation, all I’d done was some regional theater and a few commercials,” Contreras recounted. “I was living by myself in a one-bedroom on the beach in Malibu. I had just missed out on Turner and Hooch and was really bummed. So I get the script and I sit down, and I start reading it,” Contreras said. “I was blown away. I knew I could play the hell out of Snots. I just understood the character on a visceral level. I’d grown up with guys like him, so I really connected with him, you know?”

“When I got the part I was ecstatic,” Contreras remembers. “I thought I’d been handed the key to Hollywood. But then that’s all they want you to be.”

Escaping Snots’ Shadow

“After Christmas Vacation, the only scripts that came my way were for the same tired derivative version of Snots,” recalls Contreras. “Years later when I auditioned for Air Bud, the casting director said they were going to pass on me because they didn’t really see Bud as a phlegmy character. Can you believe that shit?”

Because of his difficulties landing big screen roles after Christmas Vacation, Contreras has pivoted to a career as a voice actor. And he’s grateful for the opportunities — his celebrated turn on Clifford the Big Red Dog as Clifford’s friend Ruffles won him an Emmy nod last year.

But when it comes to fans, Contreras suggested, “Ask me for my autograph. Ask me to take a picture. Let’s cover your Instagram feed. But I ain’t gonna ‘snot’ for you. That’s not who I am.”
