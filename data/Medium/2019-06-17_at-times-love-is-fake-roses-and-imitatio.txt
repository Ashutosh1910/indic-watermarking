At Times, Love is Fake Roses and Imitation Stars

Zanne Nilsson

“Good job, Zanne,” I thought the next day. “A whole month of planning and certainty and you chickened out mid-setup. Guess it’s tonight or never, or at least not for another few months until you see her again — which also could be never.”

It was about mid-afternoon at that point and I was a nervous wreck. Imagine being a nervous wreck at a lighthearted Norwegian culture festival in small-town Wisconsin. Yet there I was, on my second visit to a café that had been recommended to me by a woman at a quilting exhibition that morning, waiting for my chemo-sick girlfriend to return from the bathroom.

I was twenty-nine and in love and so very aware of death.

I’d spent all of April and half of May planning my marriage proposal to her down to the smallest detail. My therapist would tell you, as he often tells me, that I tend to deal with my persistent anxiety by over-planning things. For weeks I cut the heads off of fake roses, tested out a portable projector with rotating laser “stars” and a blue “nebula,” acquired a rose-shaped ring box and video-game-inspired ring, and separated a thousand fake rose petals by hand (which was a pain in the ass).

All of this was intended to recreate a specific scene from an anime we both liked, Revolutionary Girl Utena: The Movie. In the scene the featured (lesbian) couple, Utena and Anthy, dance together in a surreal landscape of stars and roses while the song “Toki ni Ai wa” (“At Times, Love Is”) plays. When we watched the movie Emma said she hoped to dance to that song with me someday; I’d known for a while that I wanted to propose to her, but that’s when I realized how to do it.

Sappy? Yes. Over the top? Also yes. But would she love it? I hoped so.

Because my goal wasn’t just to propose to her; I wanted to make her feel special.

She didn’t seem to think there was anything special about herself, which is absurd — she’s so incredible I can’t believe she loves me of all people. And even if she answered “no,” I wanted to at least give her a moment that would make her feel as special as she really is. If it took a grand romantic gesture to do that, well, bring it on.

Besides, what’s the point of being a ridiculous anime geek proposing to another ridiculous anime geek if you can’t do a ridiculous anime proposal?

As Emma emerged from the café bathroom and made her way to our table, I felt it again, that quiet joy and excitement that sparks inside me every time I see her. I used to think love was supposed to be painful — an iron hand squeezing all the blood out of your heart. But maybe it could feel like this too. Maybe it could always feel like this.

“I gotta do it tonight,” I thought. “I can’t chicken out again.”
