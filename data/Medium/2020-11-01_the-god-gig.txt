The GoD Gig

Justin Lee

Arc Digital presents original horror fiction for Halloween.

Last December, a friend offered me some freelance copywriting work, which I gladly accepted. He had been contracted by a couple of film producers to run a promotional campaign for their indie horror flick, Gates of Darkness (hereafter, GoD). They had secured distribution through ITN Films, which specializes in the sort of direct-to-video action and horror movies that occupy cult-actors like Steven Seagal and Lance Henrickson in their senescence. With titles like Kicking Zombie Ass for Jesus, Jurassic Bark, and their pièce de résistance of wordplay A Psycho’s Path, you know these guys don’t have a pretentious bone in their bodies. They’re in it for the pure joy of schlock. ITN was thus a natural fit for my clients’ film.

I referred to my clients as “producers,” but they’re really just money-men, which I mean as a compliment. The actual producers made an unholy mess of the production and squandered the budget before finishing. My clients were left with incomplete footage and one hell of a fiscal migraine. After shooting a few new scenes, they paid a new editor to salvage the film and he worked a small miracle: the end product is damn near coherent, contains gripping images, and has enough unintentional humor to ameliorate the schlock-lust of b-movie buffs. Hardly any budget remained for marketing, which is how they found themselves outsourcing work to lowball hacks like me.

This isn’t my first rodeo. I’ve worked for a couple major creative firms here in LA, even helmed promos for several film projects, some you’ll have heard of. But I ran afoul of some dangerous people in the industry — by which I mean I ran afoul of a coven of feminists with zero sense of humor. They whisper-networked me onto what I’ll call the “Shitty Media Men List 2.0,” which, unlike its predecessor, is encrypted. (If you’re unfamiliar with its first incarnation, God bless you.) As a result, my girlfriend of three years walked out, I got fired, and now no respectable agency will hire me. Even freelance work is difficult to snag. When you find yourself in circumstances such as these, there is no ball so low you won’t catch it.

Now, it’s not impossible to market a niche film without a budget. I managed to create a very modest buzz about GoD on a dozen or so b-movie fansites and precisely two respectable horror sites. I owe these minor successes to the fact that the film starred a few recognizable names: Tobin Bell, Adrienne Barbeau, and Alisha Boe (she’s the best thing about every scene she’s in, and she’s in most of them, despite going uncredited).

Even though I was given a screener in December, I didn’t get around to watching GoD until mid-January. I could carpet-bomb horror forums from sockpuppet accounts without having seen it, but I needed those same sockpuppets to astroturf user reviews to RottenTomatoes, IMDB, Metacritic, and a host of other sites. So I watched the film, took notes, and watched it again.

The story concerns Stephen Tade, a token teenage outcast whose black eyeliner and occult-themed piercings and jewelry seem calculated both to attract bullies and antagonize the priests and nuns who oversee his education. He hates his mom for remarrying after his father walked out on the family, and he hates his twin sister for getting along with their new step-father and step-sister. The plot thickens once Stephen begins to suspect that his father never abandoned them, but had been disappeared by evil forces within the Catholic church. As he unearths suppressed memories of sexual abuse and blasphemous violence, he is possessed by a demon seeking vengeance on the priest who preyed on Stephen as child. In the film’s climax, a good priest exorcises the demon, but not before the bad priest is dropped through a portal into hell. The film closes with Stephen reconciled to his family and the church.

GoD is no masterpiece of the macabre. Neither is it the worst movie I’ve ever seen. That distinction goes to Filthy McNasty and its sequels. Or maybe Re-Penetrator or The XXXorcist and the whole genre of horror parody porn. What I mean to say is that there’s no bottom to bad where film is concerned, but if there were, GoD would be nowhere near it. It’s just your standard teenage-angst-is-really-demonic-possession-and-some-priests-are-rapey family drama. In other words, it might have been forgettable.

Except it wasn’t. At all.

Horror movies rarely disrupt my sleep. I consider myself an imaginative person, but I rarely remember my dreams. It’s something I’ve always been uneasy about, afraid it’s evidence of a diseased psyche. But since I saw GoD, I can’t stop dreaming.

That first night I woke up so drenched in sweat I worried I’d pissed myself. During the film’s exorcism scene, the camera zooms on two weeping faces that Stephen has drawn on a bedroom wall. They’re distorted by a ripple effect, but they’re clearly meant to be the faces of suffering children. My dream contained no narrative whatsoever, just those damned eyes staring me down.

Grandpa is played by the actor John Savage, best known as the guy from The Deer Hunter who gets cuckolded after losing his legs in Nam. The buzzing I feel, it’s like someone is feeding that effect into my skin. And that odor, well, it’s how I imagine Grandpa Pedophile must smell. It’s like that greasy motherfucker is humming into my neck.
