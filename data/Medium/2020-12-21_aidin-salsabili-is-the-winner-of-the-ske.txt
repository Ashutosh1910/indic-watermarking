Aidin Salsabili is the Winner of the Sketchfab Kaiju High Poly Challenge

Mike Haggerty

Mike Haggerty: Where did you go to school, what was your experience like there? What are some of the best non-school resources you have used for learning on your own time?

Aidin Salsabili: I have a degree in art from Tehran University. But then I started reading and seeing different books and tutorials. I saw and learned almost everything I could find for 2 years. After that period, I started learning software and techniques to create game characters and props. I got acquainted with PBR texturing workflow and software. Also, one of my friends, Ehsan Ebrahimzadeh, gave me valuable tips in this regard. Ehsan is a talented artist in the industry now. We worked 14 hours a day as planned for training and progress. There is still not a day that I go to bed without learning something new in this profession.

Mike Haggerty: What were the inspirations for this piece?

Aidin Salsabili: The Kaiju are diverse and inspiring in their stories. Seeing anime and a little searching gives us interesting things. But I wanted more than that. My references are more collected than real creatures. Some bugs and some reptiles. But more than anything else, my mind is involved in building muscle movements and anatomical logic. Creating creatures is like discovering them. So what I make must be able to survive in the world of my imagination. It should move and be terrifying. Some of the creatures in the real world very inspiring.

Mike Haggerty: The Anatomy of this creature is very, very cool. There is a sense of realism to it because of the musculature you went with. Did you start from a quadruped model as a base or was this all from scratch?

Aidin Salsabili: No, I started sculpting from a simple cylinder in the ZBrush. If you start a creature like this from a basic mesh, such as a lion, you don’t permission to think creatively, and you will probably end up with a lion-like creature, not a new creature. So it is better to check the function of muscles and bones in different creatures like animals or bugs and do not use the base mesh when creating a new creature. For example, in this case, I have made his hands from a combination of Mantis hands and bird wings mechanism. But as you can see, he is neither a bird nor an insect. It is a completely new creature. Just one point I would like to add at the end. I believe that in this job we create a new world within the world in which we live.

This new world is our world and we are allowed to build anything in it and invite others to see it. It does not matter how many people see it or how many people like it. What matters is how it affects others. Some of my work, like this Kaiju, may make others eager to learn the profession or take them one step further, maybe it will spark an idea or dream.

So I try to do my best.
