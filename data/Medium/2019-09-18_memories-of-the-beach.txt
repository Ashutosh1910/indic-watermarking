Memories of the Beach

Jason Delph

The wet sand was warm under his bare feet. He stopped for a moment to look back at his solitary footprints reminiscing that it used to be two sets. A stranger stumbling upon them could see the closeness of the tracks that the two people who left them were holding hands.

A gentle wind blew from the ocean. He imagined it whispering his name. It always spoke in her beautiful voice. Quietly the wind spoke the words he yearned to hear from her; I love you. A sharp pain ripped through his chest. Tears blurred his vision.

He stopped to look out over the ocean. Here was the place where he confessed his undying love for her. She laughed when he stumbled over the words. She kissed him, his heart soared. She loved him.

They came here often for picnics or to be alone together. Here was the one little speck on the whole vast world that was theirs — a secret location containing all their memories.

Memories. He had so many of them, but so many more were slipping. Every day he struggled to remember her smile, her laugh.

He drew a circle in the wet sand with his cane. It was here that he knelt on one knee, placed the ring on her finger, and asked with as much confidence as he could muster to marry him. She couldn’t answer him, only shook her head yes while tears streamed down her lovely cheeks.

The tide rolled up to his feet, washing away his circles in the sand like so many memories easily lost in time. He sighed to himself, so many good memories and some bad.

Carefully he lowered himself down to sit. He became tired easily these days. It wasn’t so long ago that he had run along this beach. Or maybe, it has been long ago. He couldn’t remember the last time he ran on the beach.

The funeral was beautiful. So many people joined him in saying goodbye to her. The brilliant colors of the flowers were a stark contrast to the occasion. Many tears shed in anguish, but some laughter of a funny story shared by all that loved her.

She was loved by many. He loved her most of all, and she loved him.

The sunset was as brilliant as the flowers at her funeral with beautiful red and orange hues. She had loved watching the sunset here.

He was tired now, so tired. He closed his eyes to rest for a bit. He felt he needed to rest more every day.

The breeze caressed his cheek as she did at the hospital. If he kept his eyes closed, he could see her smiling. She was brave that day — more than him.

He whispered, I love you, and she whispered her love back. The gentle kiss on his lips made him smile.

“Come rest with me,” the breeze spoke in her voice.

He smiled, “Yes, my love.”

They walked hand in hand down the beach together once again.
