Lorena

unknown

She was wiping down the espresso maker as she saw the café owner walking towards her. His eyes fixed on her hand’s movements and she realised how phallic the steamer looked under his gaze. She protested by turning the hot air back on and letting it purge. Lorena wasn’t a rebel. She didn’t really have an issue with authority figures. She did have an issue with managers who threw tantrums. Steve often banged his fists on the kitchen table behind the scenes, his balding head turning beetroot red, as he cursed his way through the latest qualm. Lorena thought he looked like a leprechaun when he acted this way. She knew intuitively that Steve did this to intimidate them, especially the younger staff. He wanted to seem unhinged, so no one ever dared to ask him anything. Initially she had been confused when he had explained the simplest concepts to her, slowly and overtly worried. She caught on though, when he first said: “not to be sexist but women are always better at keeping things tidy in my experience.. so that’s why I don’t ask the guys anymore. I’ll just ask you to keep this counter all spick and span, can you do that dear?” Ah, one of those.

Not a unicorn unfortunately. Not even endangered.

The job was relatively stress-free however, and she was home in time to make a nice dinner every night. After two years in the pub, this was a welcome luxury. Lorena continued her job search on her days off but found it hard not to get discouraged. She had a master’s degree in ancient history and after a short stint as a research fellow, she had come back to Oxford. Everyone here seemed overqualified and multi-degreed and was trying to make it in this academic Hollywood just as much as she was.

When the leaves started turning, Lorena thought about how her life had tipped into the other side of her twenties. She poured the fifteenth chai latte of the day and noticed a glimpse of yellow in the corner of her eye. Chiquita, an Oxford icon, had strolled into the café with her colourful garbs and extravagant hairdo. This woman always looked as if she had a basket of fruit mysteriously tangled up in there. Lorena had seen Chiquita perform a powerful poem at an open mic once. She had talked about the word “cunt” and how it referred to “kundalini”, the female root of power. She had declared that women should reclaim the word. She had then proceeded to ask the entire audience to stand up and shout out the word together: “CUNT”. Lorena smiled. That had been a great evening, and better times.

Seeing Chiquita made her realise how there were many ways to make a difference in the world. She could become a published reference book author, and help future researchers with their work. Or she could become a kooky witch, brightening up people’s days wherever she went. Or she could go home, and make another wonderful meal for her family. She was fully grown yet so young. She wouldn’t work for Steve forever. Whatever life would throw at her, she knew she could handle it. Like a cunt.
