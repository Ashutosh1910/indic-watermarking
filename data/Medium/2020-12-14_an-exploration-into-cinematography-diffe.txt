An Exploration into Cinematography: Different styles of Cinematography that contribute to a genre and Director’s style

Caitlin Kelly

Written By Caitlin Kelly 2020

What holds a film together besides the storylines and characters? How about the camera work that contributes to enhancing the storytelling aspect? I want to analyze the different styles of cinematography that contribute to a genre or director’s style. Cinematography is a very specific art form that can be carried out differently by each cinematographer. In this piece of writing, I aim to analyze the similarities of different cinematic sequences that contribute to a genre along with the difference between each director and cinematographer’s image. The main questions I will be answering are, “what makes that cinematography unique and what’s the difference between good cinematography and poor cinematography?”, What is behind the scenes of every shot in a movie and how can this art form help expand a story? I will additionally be evaluating a specific “style” of cinematography development, and lastly, how is cinematography a specific art form that can be used to convey a certain genre and display a director’s specific style.

Genre is established not just throughout the storyline but the underlying meaning of each individual shot. A genre can be established in many different ways throughout a film production. Shot composition, lighting, and framing of certain shots have a big impact on the final product. All of these aspects are controlled by a cinematographer. In regard to careersinfilm.com, “one of the most important choices that a Cinematographer makes for every single shot is its composition — or what will be seen in it. Composition refers to how each shot is framed and all the elements within that frame. This aspect of cinematography plays a crucial role in determining what the audience knows and when they know it.” According to filmrejects.com, “if you look at the most critically acclaimed horror movies ever — Psycho, The Shining, The Exorcist, Alien, A Nightmare on Elm Street to name a few — you’ll find several commonalities of cinematography that are geared towards recreating the onscreen experience in an audience, foremost among them the films’ voyeuristic leanings. (…) The most effective way in which horror directors and cinematographers accomplish this is through how they tell the story visually, which is often a step or two ahead of how the narrative is developing.”Horror films can perfectly represent a link between cinematography and genre. For example, would the editing and lighting from a horror film still be able to play an effect on a comedy? According to “Sentai Filmworks,” “Anime is a distinct art form, but the genre is influenced by other creative mediums in terms of its depicted subjects, cultural influence and — of course — its cinematography. Many anime series are based on manga series, but that isn’t the only other art form that impacts anime today. (…) anime studios had to get creative to adapt their stories for the screen, pulling out all the cinematographic stops to render the source material effectively.” Not only just in common cinema but animation and anime across the world, the cinematography is still a huge factor that contributes to establishing a genre. “While important in all genres of film, the composition can be especially critical in horror movies. Let’s say a young teen is walking through a supposedly abandoned house by themselves. A Cinematographer might make the decision to tightly frame the Actor so that the audience has no idea of what’s around him or what danger might be lurking until it confronts him.” (careersinfilm.com)

To summarize my analysis of cinematography, it plays a crucial role in the development of the genre. Every cinematographer has a different approach to a film. In regards to this, they are telling a story behind each individual shot and set-up. Good cinematography is created through storytelling, without the script and the rest of the production. If a cinematographer is able to carry a film through mainly shots and lighting, that would be considered powerful cinematography. In conclusion, the cinematography is a crucial backbone in the creation of a film. It contributes to the main genre within a sequence of many ways (lighting, composition, etc.) Therefore, one of the main ways to convey a story through film is reliant on cinematography.
